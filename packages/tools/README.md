# @canvas/tools

## Purpose

The interaction modes: select, text, sticky, pen, shapes, lines and arrows, connectors and image placement, and the camera controller every mode shares. Tools turn pointer and keyboard input into commands and camera moves.

The camera area (`src/camera/`) is pure: the app normalises DOM events into `CameraInput`, `cameraOperationFor` maps an input to a `CameraOperation`, and `applyCameraOperation` turns a `Camera` (from `@canvas/engine`) into the next one. Nothing here touches the DOM.

The snap area (`src/snap/`) is snapping: pure rules for a dragged box or point against nearby objects' edges and centres and the grid, the guides to draw, and the R-tree query that finds the candidates. The select tool snaps its moves and resizes with it; later tools snap their creation drags with `snapPoint` and `snapBox`.

The select area (`src/select/`) is the select tool: click, shift-click, marquee and select all, with frames picked by their title, mod+click or an enclosing marquee; groups selected whole, entered with a double-click and left with Escape or a click outside; move and resize handles and rotation from a band just outside each corner (no rotate handle) on single and multi-selections with Shift constraints; arrow nudges; a move dropped over a frame takes the objects into it and one dragged out of a frame takes them onto the board, with the target frame outlined during the drag; one command, so one undo step, per gesture. Its core is pure and DOM-free: the app normalises the primary button's pointer events (and hover moves, for the cursor) into `SelectInput`, `stepSelectTool` (a state machine, `idle → pressing → dragging (move | resize | rotate | marquee) → idle`, whose state names the cursor it asks for) turns input into `SelectIntent`s against an injected `SelectHitTester`, and the geometry (`handleLayout`, `hitRotationBand`, `resizeFrame`, `rotationAngle`, `selectionFrameOf`) is exact and unit-tested. `SelectController` applies the intents to a `DocumentStore` (selection, a drag preview laid over the store, one command on release), and `SelectionOverlay` draws the frame, handles, outlines and marquee as a frame stage after the scene's `draw`.
`SelectHitTester.isAboveSelection` is required on injected hit testers. Frames with
`background: null` or alpha-zero backgrounds pass through to lower objects or the selected interior; their titles and
drop-target areas remain active. A frame above only part of a multi-selection does not block
its interior: it must paint above every selected object. This keeps the selection moving as
a unit when its frontmost object is above the frame.

The sticky area (`src/sticky/`) is the sticky tool: `StickyMode`, a click creating a 200 × 200 note centred on it in the tool's colour and editing it with the text area's `TextMode`, the prototype's six-colour palette (`NOTE_COLORS`) and the note a click makes (`newStickyObject`).

The frame area (`src/frame/`) is the frame tool: `FrameMode`, a drag drawing a frame that takes in what is wholly inside it (`planFramePlacement`) in one `createFrame`, a click a 400 × 300 one, named "Frame N" (`nextFrameName`); a frame's name is edited in place by `FrameNameMode` (`src/select/`).

The shape area (`src/shape/`) is the shape tool: `ShapeMode`, a drag drawing a shape of the tool's kind in its style (Shift 1:1, Alt from the centre, the corner snapping through `snapCreationPoint`), a click a 100-unit one, and an activated shape's label edited with the text area's `TextMode`; the shape a drag makes (`newShapeObject`, `shapeDragBox`).

The pen area (`src/pen/`) is the pen tool and vector editing, ported from the prototype (webgl-whiteboard `src/vector/network.ts`, `hit.ts`, `snap.ts`, `src/app/modes/penMode.ts` and `vectorEditMode.ts`): immutable network operations with ids from an injected source, hit tests, the 45° constraint, the pen's press and drag as pure steps, and the two `ToolMode`s, which write each gesture as one command (`createObject` for a path's first segment, then one `editNetwork` of the members the gesture changed; a chain's vertex no segment joins yet, the first click, is held in the pen and previewed, never written).

The image area (`src/image/`) is image placement and the image tools: where a dropped, pasted or picked image goes (`placeImages`), the image tool (`ImageMode`, `i`), which asks the app to open its file picker, and crop mode (`CropMode`, entered by activating an image), with its pure crop box maths in natural-size fractions (`dragCrop`) and its overlay. Uploads, the picker and the clipboard are the app's. The select tool resizes images proportionally by default and Shift frees them, the reverse of other objects (fix round 2, a controller ruling the owner may change: `resizeAspectOf`): a lone image keeps its drawn aspect (the crop region's, `croppedImageAspect`), so a box a Shift resize or a merge left off it comes back to it at the next plain resize (or crop) and the image fills its box again; a selection holding an image, or a group holding one, follows its strictest member, keeping the frame's own aspect unless Shift is held. A box off its image's aspect shows the image fitted, never stretched (`fittedImageRect`). Known limit: snapping, alignment, distribution and the selection frame of such an image use its stored box, bands included, while the hit test uses the drawn rectangle.

The line area (`src/line/`) is lines and arrows: the line tool (`LineMode`, `l`; the arrow, Shift+L, is the same tool with an arrow end cap) draws a line by drag, and a lone selected line's ends are dragged by their handles (`LineEndpoints`, which the app's select mode offers each press first). Both snap a line end to object anchors (each object's centre and edge midpoints within 8 CSS px, `nearestAnchor`), else to edges, centres and the grid (`snapPoint`), and turn it to 45° with Shift (`constrainLineEnd`); a line's ends stay free points, attached to nothing.

The draw area (`src/draw/`) is the freehand draw tool: `DrawMode` records a stroke's samples with the pen's pressure and the samples the browser coalesced into each move (`StrokeCapture`), previews it while drawn, and on release smooths, simplifies at half a screen pixel and splits it into `freehand` objects (`newFreehandObjects`), written as one `createObjects`. The engine draws freehand objects from; until then the preview shows nothing.

The connector area (`src/connector/`) is the connector tool: `ConnectorMode` draws a connector by drag through the document's `connect` command, attaching each end to an object's anchor (`nearestObjectAnchor` in `src/line/line-anchors.ts`, which lines' anchors join), to the object under the pointer with an automatic anchor, or leaving it a free point. A selected connector is framed by the route it draws now (`selectionBoxOf`), and a lone unlocked one, like a lone line, offers no rotate or resize controls.

The arrange area (`src/arrange/`) is align, distribute and tidy up: the pure planners (`planAlign`, `planDistribute`, `planTidy`, in `@canvas/document` since so the MCP server's `arrange` tool shares them) turn axis-aligned boxes into per-object moves, `planArrange` reads the selection's movable objects in their common parent's frame and turns the moves onto the board's axes, and `runArrange` runs them as one `moveObjects` command, so each operation is one undo step. The app's nine `ArrangeCommand`s (`alt+a` … `ctrl+alt+t`) run `planArrange` and `runArrange` on the select tool's selection. `carriedLabelsOf` is what "raise carries labels" means for sticky notes: the ids an order command raising a note moves, the text lying on the note included.

A selected frame with no visible background drags from its title band or selection outline,
not from its empty interior. This includes both null and alpha-zero backgrounds.

## Public API

- `TOOL_IDS`: The interaction modes of the dock: select, hand, then the creation tools.
- `ToolId`: One of `TOOL_IDS`.
- `isToolId(value)`: Whether a string names an interaction mode.
- `HandMode`, `HandModeContext`: The hand tool as a `ToolMode` (`h`): a primary-button or one-finger drag pans the view through `pan(deltaXPx, deltaYPx)`, the board following the pointer; it writes nothing, takes no key and shows an open hand (`grab`), closed while it drags (`grabbing`).
- `ToolMode`, `ToolStep`, `TOOL_STEP_CONTINUE`, `ToolPointerInput`, `ToolKeyInput`, `ToolCursor`: The tool-mode seam: a mode takes the pointer (`onPointer`: the primary button or one finger, and a secondary click and a held finger, which only the select tool uses; done, and whether select takes the input next) and the canvas keys (`onKey`), asks for a cursor, lays its fields over the overlay (`overlay()`, `toolRects` appended), follows the zoom (`onZoom`), brings its resting cursor up to date under a still pointer (`refreshCursor`); `exit()` commits. The app's `ToolRouter` routes to it.
- `chooseDefaultInk(input)`, `DefaultInkInput`, `contrastRatio(a, b)`, `DEFAULT_INK_PALETTE`, `DEFAULT_INK_CONTRAST`, `DEFAULT_CANVAS_COLORS`: The default stroke or text colour of a new object: a picked colour as is; `ink` on the empty canvas or over a pending image; else the first of `#1f1f1f`, white, black with 4.5:1 WCAG contrast against what lies under the point, translucent fills composited down to the creator's canvas; token fills under the point resolve with `tokens`, the board's table. Pure.
- `colorsUnderPoint(objects, point, averageColorOf?, colors?)`, `UnderColor`, `AverageColorOf`, `UnderPointColors`: The filled objects under a board point, topmost first in paint order, each with its colour there (shapes by their kind's outline, paths by even-odd, gradients by `paintColorAt`, stickies, frames, image averages), down to the first opaque one; colour tokens resolve in each object's `effectiveColorMode` with the viewer's theme and the board's token table (`colors`; the light theme's built-ins when absent). Pure.
- `creationColor(store, point, styleColor, source?, tool?)`, `CreationInkSource`, `copyInkableColor(color)`: What the creation tools store: a tool style's picked colour (a tuple, or `ink` the person picked for that tool, `CreationInkSource.isInkPicked`), or its default `ink` resolved by `chooseDefaultInk` where the creation starts; the shell's theme and canvas colour for it (the contexts' `creationInk`).
- `applyCameraOperation(camera, operation)`, `CameraOperation`: The camera controller: `panBy`, `zoomAt` (the point stays fixed), `zoomTo`, `fitToRect`, `fitToSelection`, `resetToOrigin`; pure, zoom clamped to 0.02×–64×.
- `cameraOperationFor(input, context)`, `CameraInput`, `CameraInputContext`, `PanDirection`, `KeyZoomCommand`: The input mapper: Control/⌘-wheel zooms about the cursor, wheel pans, pinch zooms about its midpoint, drag pans, arrows pan, zoom keys zoom and fit.
- `KEYBOARD_PAN_PX`, `KEYBOARD_PAN_LARGE_PX`, `FIT_PADDING_PX`: Arrow pan (100 px, 500 px with Shift), the fit margin (48 px).
- `ZOOM_LEVELS`, `steppedZoom(zoom, direction)`: The zoom ladder (10% to 3200%) that zoom in and zoom out step through, and the next level up or down from any zoom, the camera's limits past its ends.
- `panCamera`, `zoomCameraAt`, `zoomCameraTo`, `fitCameraToRect`, `resetCameraToOrigin`, `resizeCameraViewport`: The pure camera moves behind the operations, and following a viewport resize (top-left and zoom kept).
- `ScreenPoint`, `ViewportSize`: A point in CSS px on the viewport; a viewport's CSS size and device pixel ratio.
- `TwoFingerGesture`: Tracks touch pointers; two fingers' moves become `drag` (midpoint) and `pinch` (span ratio) input.
- `CameraTransition`, `CameraTransitionOptions`, `interpolateCamera(from, to, progress)`: The eased, inertia-free animation of a fit, sampled against an injected clock; zoom interpolates geometrically, the viewport centre linearly.
- `SelectionState`, `EMPTY_SELECTION`, `selectionOf`, `toggleSelected`, `isSelected`, `withoutObjects`, `isSameSelection`: The selection: an ordered set of ids and the primary one; pure operations (click, shift-click, deleted objects leave).
- `OrientedBox`, `boxPointToWorld`, `worldPointToBox`, `boxCornersOf`, `boxCentreOf`, `isInsideBox`, `boxAround`, `rotateBoxAbout`, `translateBox`, `rotatePointAbout`, `normalizeAngle`: A rectangle turned about its top-left corner (the selection frame) and the pure geometry on it.
- `SelectionSource`, `orientedBoxOf`, `localRectOf`, `selectionBoxOf`, `selectionFrameOf`, `selectTargetOf`, `transformableIds`, `isResizable`: What the tool reads from the document; an object's turned box (a group's from its children) and its bounds in its own frame; one object's box as the selection outlines it (`selectionBoxOf`: a connector by where it is drawn;'s peer selections use it) or a multi-selection's axis-aligned frame; the outermost group not entered that a click selects (`selectTargetOf(id, source, enteredGroupId)`); what a transform moves (no locked objects, no group holding one, no child of a moved parent); whether every movable object has a box or is a group. An object the engine does not draw has no box: it is left out of the frame, outlines and snapping, and stays selectable by id and deletable. The source's optional `isDrawable(id)` gives the app's hit gate's verdict (`SelectControllerOptions.isDrawable`), so the frame agrees with the picture; without it, a box that is not finite, has a negative size or lies past `DRAWABLE_EXTENT` is refused on its own (`drawable-box.ts`). The line tool's end handles and a mode's activation are refused for an undrawable object too.
- `selectionScopeOf(ids, source, knownFar?)`, `SelectionScope`, `nearIds`, `farIds`, `farSkipOf`, `farSkipOfScope`, `FarSkip`: Far objects in a selection (`isFarObject` of `@canvas/document`: a box on the board, its own or a descendant's, past the board bound or not computable). The scope holds the far ones and the near ones a transform changes, worked out once per selection and kept by the frame cache; a transform skips the far ones and `farSkipOf` names them (`ids`) and says whether that was all; `SelectControllerOptions.onFarSkipped` reports it. `SelectionScope.near` and `nearIds` also leave out the objects the engine does not draw (`SelectionSource.isDrawable`): one rule, `isLeftOutOfTransforms` (`transform-exclusion.ts`), decides for every transform (move, nudge, rotate, resize, align, distribute, tidy) and for the resize handles, so the rest of the selection moves instead of the command being refused whole; Delete keeps both kinds (`transformableIds`). `SelectionScope.skipped`, `farIds` and the one notice (`farSkipOf`, `onFarSkipped`) count both kinds. Known limit: a group whose subtree holds an undrawable object is itself drawable, so it still offers resize handles, and `resizeObjects`, which validates every descendant it rewrites, refuses the whole resize with a notice (moves are unaffected: only the group is rewritten).
- `enteredChainOf`, `scopeOfTarget`, `outerGroupOf`, `enteredScopeIds`, `isInsideEnteredGroup`, `selectionOnEntering`: The entered-group scope (shared with select same type): the entered group and the groups above it, the level a press takes the tool to, the level Escape leaves to, the entered group's children (what select all and select same type search), whether an object is inside it (what a marquee takes), what entering selects.
- `DOUBLE_PRESS_INTERVAL_MS`, `DOUBLE_PRESS_DISTANCE_PX`, `DOUBLE_TAP_DISTANCE_PX`, `doublePressDistancePx(pointerType)`, `isDoublePress`, `PressStamp`: A double press: two primary presses within 400 ms and 4 CSS px, timed by the inputs' timestamps. A finger's double press (a double-tap) allows 32 CSS px instead of 4, in the same 400 ms, and a finger's presses on empty board pair too (`PressStamp.id` null).
- `RESIZE_HANDLES`, `ResizeHandle`, `HandlePlacement`, `handleLayout(frame, zoom, canResize)`, `hitHandle(frame, zoom, point, canResize, pointerType?)`, `HANDLE_SIZE_PX`, `HANDLE_HIT_SIZE_PX`, `TOUCH_HANDLE_HIT_SIZE_PX`, `handleHitSizePx(pointerType)`, `TOUCH_HANDLE_INWARD_REACH_PX`, `touchInwardReachPx(sidePx)`, `touchCornerOutwardReachPx(frame, zoom)`: The eight resize handles in world units at one screen size (8 px drawn, 12 px pickable); the handle under a point, corners before edges. There is no rotate handle. A finger's squares are 44 px, reaching at most 6 px (or a sixth of the frame's on-screen side) into the frame and the rest out of it.
- `SELECTION_CORNERS`, `SelectionCorner`, `hitRotationBand(frame, zoom, point, pointerType?)`, `ROTATION_BAND_WIDTH_PX`, `TOUCH_ROTATION_BAND_WIDTH_PX`, `rotationBandWidthPx(pointerType)`: The rotation bands: a quarter ring outside each corner of the frame, turned with it, from the corner handle's pickable edge (6 px) to 12 CSS px further out at every zoom; the corner whose band holds a point. For a finger the band starts where the corner's touch square ends (38 to 44 px out) and is 24 px wide.
- `resizeFrame(frame, handle, delta, isProportional, aspect?)`, `MIN_RESIZE_SIZE`: A handle drag along the frame's own axes; a proportional resize keeps the frame's aspect ratio, or the aspect given (a lone image's drawn aspect); edges stop 1 world unit short of each other.
- `resizeAspectOf(ids, source, near?)`, `isProportionalResize(rule, isShiftPressed)`, `ResizeAspect`, `FREE_RESIZE`: How a resize keeps its aspect (fix round 2): images proportionally by default, at their drawn aspect (the crop region's) when alone, with Shift freeing them; a selection holding an image, or a group holding one, follows it; other objects are free, Shift constraining them.
- `rotationAngle(pivot, from, to, frameRotation, isSnapped)`, `ROTATION_SNAP_STEP`: The turn a rotation drag makes about the pivot; Shift lands the frame on 15° steps.
- `SelectHitTester`, `PointHit`, `createSelectHitTester(index, source)`: The injected hit tester and its implementation over the engine's `hitTopmost` (point and frame picks, top down to the first hit) and `hitRect` (marquee): a press lands on an object, a frame's title band or a frame's background (`PointHit.part`); groups selected whole until entered (both take the entered group; `PointHit.scope` is the level a press takes the tool to, and a marquee in an entered group takes only what is inside it); a marquee takes a frame only when it encloses it and leaves out locked objects and children of taken parents; `frameAt` finds the top-most accepted frame under a point, where a moved object lands. `isAboveSelection` compares paint order so an unrelated front frame’s background starts a marquee even inside a selected shape or multi-selection; a selected object remains draggable within its own ancestor frame.
- `MarqueeHits`, `MarqueeSelect`: one marquee drag's hits kept between pointer moves, re-testing only the objects in the strips between the last rectangle and the new one, plus any whose geometry reaches past their indexed box; `SelectHitTester.marquee` makes one.
- `FRAME_TITLE_BAND_PX`, `isOnFrameTitle(frame, source, point, zoom)`, `frameTitleAt(index, source, point, zoom)`: A frame's title band, 24 CSS px tall above its top edge across its width, turned with the frame: where its name goes and where a click picks it; the topmost frame whose band holds a point, among the frames the caller accepts (the hit tester accepts a band over a frame's background only when that frame holds the band's frame); a frame whose name is hidden (`isFrameNameShown`) has no band.
- `isAboveFrameTop(frame, source, point)`: Whether a point lies above a frame's top edge in its own frame: a double press that activated the frame landed on its title band.
- `isMarqueeEligible(id, rect, mode, source)`: Whether a marquee may take a select target: a frame only when enclosed (either direction), a group on touch dragging right and enclosed dragging left, anything else as the engine found it.
- `SelectInput`, `SelectPointer`, `SelectPointerType`, `PointerSample`, `NudgeDirection` (`enter` activates the one selected object): Normalised input: primary-button down/move/up (a move with no press is a hover) with screen and world points, Shift and mod (⌘ or Control), the event's timestamp and the snap modifier (`isSnapSuspended`: Alt, Option on a Mac), pointer cancel, Escape, arrow nudges. Pointer input also carries its pointer type; a secondary click is `contextPress`, a held finger `hold` (the time and where the finger is), and a second finger a `pointerCancel`. A pointer carries a pen's `pressure`, and a pressed move its `trail`, the samples coalesced into it in world units (`PointerSample`).
- `SelectIntent`, `SelectActivation` (`activate`: a double press, or Enter with one object selected): What the machine asks for: `select`, `toggle`, `marquee`, `move`, `resize`, `rotate`, `nudge`, `commit`, `cancel`. Also `contextMenu` (a long-press or a secondary click) and `doubleTap` (a finger's double-tap on empty board: zoom to fit the selection or the board).
- `stepSelectTool(state, input, context)`, `SelectToolState`, `SelectToolStep`, `SelectToolContext`, `PressTarget`, `SelectDrag`, `SelectCursor`, `IDLE_SELECT_TOOL`, `CLICK_THRESHOLD_PX`, `clickThresholdPx(pointerType)`, `NUDGE_DISTANCE`, `NUDGE_LARGE_DISTANCE`: The pure state machine: resize handles before rotation bands before objects and frame titles before empty space (a frame's background counts as empty unless mod is held, or unless that frame is already selected, when a drag from it moves the selection and a click keeps it); a 4 px click threshold; marquee `intersect` dragging right, `contain` dragging left; nudges of 1 and 10 world units; its `cursor` is a `RotateCursor` over a band and through a rotation drag, else `default`. A finger waits 8 px before it drags; a touch press held 500 ms in place, or a secondary click, opens the context menu and leaves the tool at rest.
- `RotateCursor`, `rotateCursorStepOf(angle)`, `ROTATE_CURSOR_STEPS`: The rotate cursor turned to match its corner: `angle`, radians clockwise, is the corner's quarter turn (top-left 0) plus the frame's rotation; the step (0–23) quantising it to 15°, for the app's one cursor image per step.
- `TransformPlan`, `BoxPlacement`, `planTransform(intent, ids, source)`, `previewObjects(plan, source)`, `runTransformPlan(store, origin, environment, plan)`: A gesture as its one command (`moveObjects`, `resizeObjects`, `rotateObjects`): the preview is exactly what the command writes (a group resize previews every descendant through the command's own `planGroupResize`), apart from a move's reparents (`parentIds`), which are added at release.
- `selectSameTypeOf(selection, board, scope)`, `SameTypeSource`: Select same type: the selection extended with every unlocked object of its types (a shape of the same kind too) among the scope's children (the root, or an entered group) and its frames' at any depth, never a group's children; the same selection when nothing is added.
- `ArrangeFrame`, `arrangeFrameOf(ids, source)`: The movable selection (no locked objects, no child of a selected parent, only objects with a box) as boxes in the common parent's frame, or the board's for mixed parents; the parent frame's own rectangle.
- `ARRANGE_OPERATIONS`, `ArrangeOperation`, `isArrangeOperation(value)`: The nine operations, named as the app's `ArrangeCommand`s.
- `ArrangePlan`, `planArrange(operation, ids, source)`, `runArrange(store, origin, environment, plan)`: An operation as board-space moves (a single object aligns to its parent frame, else nothing), and the one `moveObjects` command that runs it, with no reparenting.
- `SelectController`, `SelectControllerOptions`: Runs the tool against a `DocumentStore`: applies intents (an `activate` on a group not entered enters it, `enterGroup`; any other goes to `onActivate`; `enteredGroupId` is the group entered), shows drags through `source` (a preview over the store), runs one command per gesture, nudges, deletes, selects all, follows deletions, and extends the selection to its types (`selectSameType(scope)`, no command; the scope defaults to the entered group); `cursor` is the tool's cursor for the app to show, and `refreshCursor(world, zoom)` brings it up to date when the camera moves under a still pointer. `handle(input, zoom, camera?, snapping?)` snaps moves and resizes against `snapIndex` when given the person's `SnapSettings`, within the camera's view; `onCommandRefused` receives the errors of a refused gesture command (a refused drop is then made as a plain move). `onContextMenu` is asked to open the context menu at a point; `onDoubleTap(fit)` to zoom where a finger's double-tap activates nothing (`canActivate` says which objects have a mode of their own).
- `LONG_PRESS_MS`, `LONG_PRESS_SLOP_PX`, `isLongPress`, `TouchCallbacks`, `DoubleTapFit`: Touch timing: a long-press is 500 ms within 8 CSS px. `TouchCallbacks`: the controller's `onContextMenu` (told whether a finger's long-press asked), `onDoubleTap` and `canActivate`.
- `SelectionOverlayModel` (with `toolRects`, the active mode's rectangles, drawn last), `EMPTY_OVERLAY`, `OverlayRect`, `overlayRectsOf(model, zoom)`, `SELECTION_COLOR`, `HANDLE_FILL`, `MARQUEE_FILL`, `ENTERED_GROUP_COLOR`, `OUTLINE_WIDTH_PX`, `LOCKED_COLOR`, `LOCK_BADGE_SIZE_PX`, `LOCK_BADGE_GAP_PX`: What the overlay shows and the flat rectangles that draw it at a zoom (1 px outlines, white handles with a blue border, the drop target's 1 px outline during a move; a locked selection (`isLocked`) in grey with a lock badge and no handles; a capped selection's count badge on top, in the lock colour when locked), the entered group's half-strength outline, the snapping guides (`guides`, 1 px red lines over the frame), and the tool mode's rectangles last.
- `SelectionStyle`, `SELECTION_FRAME_GAP_PX`, `SELECTION_FRAME_WIDTH_PX`, `HANDLE_EDGE_PX`: The whiteboard design's selection (`OverlayPalette.selection`, sent only under the floating chrome): a 2 px outline 2 px outside the frame and 8 px handles 3 px out of its corners and edges with a 1.5 px edge, both in `frameColor`, round `handleFill`, and the overlay's other selection marks (each outline of a multi-selection, the marquee, the drop target, the insertion line, the count badge) in `color`.
- `OverlayPalette`, `textHighlightOf(palette)`, `TEXT_HIGHLIGHT_ALPHA`: The editing states' colours for the viewer's theme, which the shell sends with the overlay's model (`palette`) and gives the text mode (`CreationInkSource.overlayPalette`): `you` (the edited text's outline and highlight), `guide` (guides and gap runs), `hover`, `locked` (the dashed lock outline). Without one, the overlay's classic colours.
- `OverlayLabel`, `OverlayLabelKind`, `sizeLabelOf(frame)`, `extentLabelOf(box)`, `gapLabelsOf(gaps)`, `lockedLabelOf(frame)`, `connectLabelOf(point)`, `LOCKED_LABEL_TEXT`, `CONNECT_LABEL_TEXT`: The editing states' text: the size readout of a resize or of a shape being drawn, each gap run's length, "Locked · can't move or resize", "Release to connect" while a connector's end is over an object (whose outline the connector tool draws in the palette's `you`); world-anchored, in the model's `labels`, drawn by the shell as pills (the GPU overlay ignores them). Pure.
- `dashedOutlineOf(box, t, zoom, color, window?)`, `dashWindowOf(visible)`, `MAX_LOCKED_DASHES`, `gapRunOf(gap, t, zoom, color)`, `LOCKED_DASH_PX`, `LOCKED_DASH_GAP_PX`, `GAP_TICK_PX`: The editing states' marks: a locked selection's dashed outline, cut only in a window round the view (`dashWindowOf`, which holds still through a pan inside its margin) and drawn solid past `MAX_LOCKED_DASHES`, and a gap run with its end ticks, one screen size at any zoom. Pure.
- `connectorEndRects(end, source, zoom)`, `connectorTargetRects(end, source, zoom, palette)`: What the connector tool shows for an end: handles on the anchors of the object it is on and a marker on the anchor it landed on; and, with the shell's palette, the object a dragged end would attach to outlined in its `you`. Pure.
- `MAX_OUTLINED_OBJECTS`, `selectionOutlinesOf(ids, boxOf)`, `SelectionOutlines`: The outline cap (500) and the selection's part of the overlay model: each object's box for a multi-selection up to the cap, the selection's count above it (O(1), no box read).

- `isLockedSelection(ids, source)`, `lockToggleOf(ids, source)`, `LockChange`: Whether every selected object is locked; the one `setLocked` input lock or unlock (mod+shift+l) makes: unlock a wholly locked selection, else lock every object.
- `orderTargetOf(ids, source)`, `OrderTarget`: The objects an order command moves: the selection's largest same-parent subset (a tie goes to the primary's parent), and the objects it leaves.
- `SelectionOverlay`, `SELECTION_OVERLAY_STAGE_NAME`: Draws the overlay through the solid-rect pipeline in its own render pass after `draw`, rebuilt only when the model or zoom changes.
- `createSelectionOverlayLayer(backend)`, `isSelectionOverlayModel(value)`: The overlay as an engine host's `EngineOverlay` layer: the app's engine worker and main-thread host draw the model the page sends with `setOverlay`; the guard refuses a model that is not one.
- `wheelDeltaPx(delta, deltaMode, pageHeightPx)`, `LINE_HEIGHT_PX`: A wheel delta in CSS px whatever its `deltaMode` (pixels, 16 px lines, pages).
- `snapBox(box, candidates, thresholdWorld, grid?, edges?)`, `SnapResult`, `SnapEdge`, `SnapEdges`, `SnapGrid`, `ALL_SNAP_EDGES`, `guidesOf(box, candidates, edges)`: Snapping a dragged box: per axis, the nearest edge or centre of a candidate within the threshold, else the grid (edges only); objects win; the offset and the guides the moved box shares with candidates. ## Design notes

- **Zoom about a point** is the prototype's `zoomAt` (webgl-whiteboard `src/render/camera.ts`) in this camera's form, where `(x, y)` is the world point at the viewport's top-left: the world point under the cursor is computed at the old zoom and put back under the cursor at the new, clamped zoom.
- **Wheel zoom** uses `exp(−deltaY / 100)`: the prototype's rule, and exactly the inverse of how Chrome encodes a trackpad pinch as Control-wheel events, so a pinch zooms by its own scale.
- **Two-finger touch** compares the pair of fingers before and after each move: the midpoint's shift pans and the span's ratio zooms about the new midpoint, so the world point under the fingers stays under them. One finger is left to the active tool.
- **Zoom to fit** frames the board with a 48 px margin, or returns to the origin on an empty board; **zoom to selection** does nothing when nothing is selected. Fits animate through `CameraTransition` (none of it runs on a timer here: the caller samples it each frame); a zero duration, for reduced motion, jumps.

- **Selecting** follows common tools: a press on an unselected object selects it (Shift adds it) at once, so a drag moves it; a press on a selected one waits for the release, so a drag moves the whole selection and a click narrows it (Shift removes it). Handles, then the rotation bands, take priority over objects; a press on empty space inside a multi-selection's frame moves the selection; on empty board a drag draws a marquee, which touches (`intersect`) dragging right and encloses (`contain`) dragging left. A grouped object selects its outermost group not entered (below, "Entering groups"); locked objects can be clicked but not marqueed or moved. The prototype's raise-on-select (webgl-whiteboard `src/app/controller.ts`, `select`) is not ported: selecting is not a document change and must not make an undo step; z-order has its own commands.
- **Frames** hold other objects, so their background is where people start marquees over what is inside. A plain press on the background of a frame that is not selected is not a hit: it behaves exactly as on empty board, so a drag draws a marquee over the frame's children and a click clears the selection. The frame itself is picked by a click on its title band (`FRAME_TITLE_BAND_PX`, 24 CSS px above its top edge, across its width, turned with it), by a mod+click (⌘ or Control) on its background, or by a marquee that encloses it; a drag from the title moves it. A marquee never takes a frame it only touches, whichever way it is dragged: it takes the children it reaches, by the usual touch-right, enclose-left rule; an enclosed frame carries its children, so they are not listed with it. Objects win over a title band they cover; a title band wins over empty board and over the background of a frame that holds its frame (a nested frame's title over its parent), but never over another frame's background, since a band sized on screen reaches over neighbouring frames when zoomed out. A drag from the background of a frame that is already selected, alone or with others, moves the selection instead of drawing a marquee (with Shift it is still an additive marquee; a click there leaves the selection as it is). A locked frame can be clicked by its title but is never marqueed, and does not hide its children from a marquee. The engine draws each frame's name in the band (the frame title pass of `@canvas/engine`, whose `FRAME_TITLE_BAND_PX` must stay equal to this one); the overlay is unchanged.
- **Select same type** extends the selection with every unlocked object whose type is one of the selected objects' types, a shape only with shapes of its kind (rectangles with rectangles, not ellipses). Candidates are the scope's children and, through every frame among them, the frames' children at any depth, found in one pass (a frame's background is where people work, so its contents count as the board's); a locked frame is not taken but, as with a marquee, does not hide its children. A group is selected whole, so its children are never taken on their own, unless the caller passes the group as the scope (an entered group). Objects already selected stay, locked ones included, and keep the primary. It changes only the selection, which is not document state: no command, no change set, no undo step.
- **Far objects**. An object is far when its box on the board, or the box of anything it carries, has an extent past the board bound (±2^30), or a number it needs is not finite, or its box cannot be worked out (`isFarObject` in `@canvas/document`, the one test the commands' bound, the tool and the sync fuzz share). Storing a number past the bound does not make it far: a member of a group of two frames at -B and +B stores 2B and drags like any other. A transform (a drag's release, a nudge, an align, distribute or tidy) applies to the near objects and skips the far ones; the selection frame the handles sit on, and so the pivot and origin, are computed from the near objects only, and the controller calls `onFarSkipped({ ids, isEvery })` once so the shell can say so; when the command is refused the refusal is the one notice. When every object is far the transform does nothing and `isEvery` says so. The near and far objects are worked out once per selection (`SelectionScope`, kept by `SelectionFrameCache` until a change set touches the selection, a selected group's or frame's descendants included, so a peer moving a child of a selected frame past the bound makes the frame far at the next transform), so a pointer move of a drag does not test them again (`select-controller.bench.ts`, a drag of 10,000 selected objects). Delete does not skip them: a far object can always be deleted, and moved `to` a position inside the bound by a command. The pivot and origin the tool sends are plain points: the command judges where the objects land, not them. Tested in `far-selection.test.ts`, `select-controller-far.test.ts` and `select-controller-world.test.ts`.
- **Lock**. A locked object is picked by a click (so it can be restyled, reordered and unlocked) but never by a marquee (`hitRect`) or select all, and never moved, resized, rotated or reparented: `transformableIds` leaves it out of every gesture's plan, and a gesture with nothing left to move keeps the frame where the objects are. When every selected object is locked (`isLockedSelection`, cached with the frame), the tool context's `isLocked` removes the resize handles and rotation bands, and the overlay draws the frame in `LOCKED_COLOR` with a lock badge, a 14 px square just right of the frame's top-right corner holding a white padlock, sized on screen like the handles. The badge is drawn from the flag at each frame's zoom rather than stored as world-sized rectangles in the model, so it keeps its screen size while the camera zooms without the model being sent again.
- **Read-only**. `SelectControllerOptions.canEdit`, read at every input (and `SelectController.canEdit`), makes the select tool a viewer's: it still selects (a click, Shift, a marquee, select all), but its frame offers no handles and no rotation bands (`hasFrameControls` false in its context, `canResize` false in its overlay), and `readOnlyIntentsOf` drops every transform and nudge and turns a gesture's commit into a cancel, so a viewer's press, drag or arrow key writes nothing. An `activate` still reaches the app, whose router opens no editor for a viewer. `ToolMode.abandon` (optional) ends a mode writing nothing, for an editor made a viewer mid-edit: the text mode drops held text and keeps an emptied text (`TextSession.end(false)`), the pen and vector editing leave a path as written (`PathSession.abandon`), crop drops its draft, and the shape and sticky modes abandon their label or note editing; a mode without it ends with `exit`.
- **Retained root move previews.** `RootMovePreview` keeps stable root-leaf membership and base geometry, computes the same absolute double-precision positions as ordinary `previewObjects`, and materializes objects only when consumers request them. Nested objects, containers and unsupported membership retain ordinary previews. A document change affecting the selection materializes the existing preview before replacing its retained state.

- **One command per gesture.** A drag never writes to the document: each move plans the gesture's command (`planTransform`), and `previewObjects` gives the objects exactly as that command will leave them, which the scene draws through `SelectController.source`. The release runs the one command, so a gesture is one transaction, one change set and one undo step; Escape or a lost pointer drops the preview and writes nothing. The drag itself (plan, preview, moved frame, drop target and the command on release) is `SelectGesture` (`select-gesture.ts`, internal), split out of `SelectController` in so the controller keeps the selection, the cache, the cursor and the selection commands.
- **The frame.** One object's frame is its own box turned with it, so its handles resize along its own axes; several objects share the axis-aligned box round all of theirs. A multi-selection resize moves each object's centre with the frame and stretches it along its own axes, keeping its rotation (exact for unturned objects and quarter turns). Resize handles show only when every movable object stores a box or is a group (`resizeObjects` refuses lines, paths and connectors); rotation works on anything. A group's box is its derived box (`localRectOf`), placed in a resized frame like any other, and its resize scales what it holds (the document's `planGroupResize`: boxed descendants stretch along the group's axes and move with it, line points and path vertices scale exactly, font sizes do not change), in one `resizeObjects` command; the preview runs the same planner, so it is what the command writes. A group holding a locked object is not transformable (a locked child locks its group's transform; the commands refuse it too): moves, resizes, rotations, arrangement and Delete leave it (`transformableIds`; the controller's `deleteSelection` returns those groups so the app can say why), but a marquee takes it, as a click does (`unlockedTopmostIds`). A lone group whose derived box has no length along an axis cannot scale along it: `isResizable` then offers only the other axis's edge handles (`ResizeAbility` `'x'` or `'y'`), or none.
- **Entering groups**. The tool's state keeps the innermost entered group, `enteredGroupId`, which every phase carries; the groups above it, as long as its parents are groups, count as entered too, read from the document each time (`entered-group.ts`), so the scope cannot go stale. A click selects the outermost group that is not entered (`selectTargetOf`), and a press takes the tool to the level of what it lands on (`PointHit.scope`): a press outside the entered group leaves it (all the way, or to the outer entered group the object sits in), a click on empty board leaves one level and clears the selection, and Escape at rest leaves one level and selects the group it left. A marquee started inside an entered group takes only what is inside it, at its level; select all takes its unlocked children. A double press, two primary presses within 400 ms and 4 CSS px by the inputs' timestamps (`isDoublePress`; the second press does not act as a click, and a third press is not another double), emits the `activate` intent with the object pressed at the tool's level (only presses on an object are remembered, and the two presses must land on the same one); the controller enters a group that way (`enterGroup`: the object under the pointer at the group's level is selected, else its unlocked children) and hands every other activation to `onActivate` for the app's tool modes. A group entered this way is left when a collaborator deletes or ungroups it. The overlay outlines the entered group's derived box in the selection colour at half strength, under everything else (`ENTERED_GROUP_COLOR`); the box is kept between inputs by `EnteredGroupOutline`, a `SelectionFrameCache` over the one group, so a hover inside a 1,000-child group costs what it costs at the top level (`select-controller.bench.ts`). Dragging inside an entered group moves its children and never reparents them (an object whose parent is a group is never dropped); dragging the whole group across a frame's edge takes the group into the frame. The select tool's state machine detects the double press itself (`double-press.ts`).
- **Rotation** starts from a band just outside each corner of the frame, as in Figma (had a rotate handle above the frame, which is gone). Each band is a quarter ring in the quadrant beyond both of the corner's edges, turned with the frame, from the corner handle's pickable edge (6 CSS px from the corner) to 12 CSS px further out, divided by the zoom like the handles; it never reaches inside the frame. A press resolves the corner's resize handle first where the two overlap, then the band, then whatever is under the pointer, so the band wins over an object beside the selection. A drag from a band turns the selection about its frame's centre exactly as the handle did (Shift snaps to 15°, one command per gesture). The bands are not drawn; the tool's state asks for a rotate cursor over them and through the drag, which the app shows, and hover moves (no button down) only update that cursor. The cursor turns to match its corner, as Figma's does: its angle is the corner's quarter turn (top-left 0°, top-right 90°, bottom-right 180°, bottom-left 270°, as the icon's arc wraps each) plus the frame's rotation, so the four corners always read exactly a quarter turn apart whatever the box's aspect ratio or where the pointer is in the band, and a turned selection turns all four. Through a rotation drag it is the starting corner's angle on the frame turned by the angle swept about the pivot so far (before any Shift snap), so it turns with the selection. The cursor object (and with it the idle state) is kept while the angle stays in the same 15° step (`rotateCursorStepOf`), so a hover allocates nothing until the orientation changes and the app rewrites the style only then. A hover must stay constant time with any selection, so the controller keeps the selection's frame and resize flag (`SelectionFrameCache`) until the selection changes, a gesture ends, or a change set touches what the frame depends on: a selected object, one of its ancestors, or anything under a selected group (`isChangeRelated`, O(1) per touched id, plus an O(depth) walk up the parents of created and updated objects only while a group is selected). Other change sets, which with twenty collaborators arrive hundreds of times a second, are ignored outright: no recompute, no document read, no change callback. The preview is not read, so it never clears the cache. The resting cursor is recomputed for the last pointer when the frame changes or the camera does, so a zoom, an undo, a remote edit to the selection or the axis-aligned frame after a multi-selection turn never leave it stale (`select-controller.bench.ts`: a hover, and 400 unrelated change sets, with 10,000 objects selected).
- **Dropping into frames**. On release of a move drag, `withDropTargets` gives each moved object the top-most frame under the centre of its box after the move (`SelectHitTester.frameAt` over the document's index), or the root when there is none, and the gesture's one `moveObjects` command carries a `parentId` for each object whose parent changes: the command converts its position and rotation into the new frame (turned and nested frames included) and puts it on top of its new siblings, so the move and the reparent are one undo step. Frames that move with the gesture (the moved objects and anything inside them) take nothing, since the index still has them where they were; an object whose parent is a group moves with its group and is never dropped; an object whose centre stays in its own frame keeps it. Keyboard nudges keep the parent; only a pointer drag reparents. If the document refuses the drop (a target frame whose top child has a stacking key a peer wrote that is not a fractional-index key), the refused command wrote nothing, so `runDropPlan` makes the same move without the drops: the objects stay where the pointer put them in their old parents, still one change set, and `SelectControllerOptions.onCommandRefused` receives the errors. During the drag the preview keeps every object in its parent (only the position changes), and the overlay outlines the frame the primary object (or else the first moved one) would land in, including its own frame while it stays inside (`dropTargetBox`, O(depth) and one point query per pointer move). The reparent's change set touches the moved objects, so `SelectionFrameCache` records their new ancestors: a later edit to the new frame moves the selection frame, one to the old frame is ignored.
- **Snapping**. Each move or resize step's intent passes through `snapTransform` in `SelectGesture.update` (the gesture holds the `DragSnapper`; the controller hands it each input's snap modifier, zoom, the camera's view and the person's `SnapSettings`) before `planTransform`, so the preview, the overlay's frame and the gesture's one command all carry the snapped displacement; the command's shape is unchanged and one undo step undoes it. What snaps is the frame of the selection's movable objects (locked ones are left out; a wholly locked selection snaps nothing and shows no guide). A move snaps that frame's bounds, translated by the drag, by its edges and centre (only along its axis when Shift locks it); a resize snaps only the edges its handle drags (the others stay put), and a Shift resize lets the edge nearer a line lead while the other side follows the ratio. A frame at 90°, 180° or 270° has its edges on the world's axes, so it resizes with snapping by the world edges its handle drags; a single object turned any other way resizes without snapping, which is accepted for now. On each axis the nearest candidate line within 6 CSS px wins (`snapThresholdAt`: the threshold divided by the zoom); failing that, the nearest grid line through one of the box's edges, but only while the dot grid shows: grid snapping follows the grid's visibility, so a box lands on dots one can see, at the spacing they are drawn at (`gridSpacingAt`). The candidates come from six thin R-tree queries, a strip within reach of each of the box's six lines, clipped to the viewport: exactly the objects with a line that can meet one of the box's, however large the box, and nothing off screen; the dragged objects and everything inside them are left out, and each axis keeps its 64 nearest (`SNAP_CANDIDATES_PER_BAND`), ranked by the index's own boxes, which bounds a step's geometry and ranking work; the walk up each hit's parents that leaves out what moves runs before the cap, once per object the index returns. A box more than half the viewport wide or tall snaps to no object, whether a selection (a select-all drag at fit) or a single object that large. Inside an entered group, snapping still considers every object on screen, not only the group's children. The kept boxes are cached for the gesture, since neighbours do not move while one drags. A step's cost is the six queries plus a constant and a parent walk per object they return (those lined up with the box), then O(N log N) for the N ≤ 128 kept: `snap.bench.ts` holds every case under 1 ms a step with 10,000 objects on screen (one object, a 10 × 10 block, the widest block that still snaps, everything selected at fit, a resize). The guides are every line the snapped box shares with a candidate, spanning both; the grid draws none. Holding Alt (Option on a Mac; Control is mod there) suspends snapping, and pressing or releasing it (or Shift) with the pointer still takes the step again at once; the app passes the person's preferences: object snapping on by default, the grid, and with it grid snapping, off.
- **The overlay** is flat rectangles through the solid-rect pipeline, in a render pass of its own after the scene's `draw`, so it is always on top; widths are divided by the zoom so outlines stay 1 px and handles 8 px on screen (the prototype drew its outline and handles as rectangles too). Rectangles draw in slot order (a handle's white inside over its blue square), so the slots stay 0, 1, 2, …: the unused tail is released highest first, since the instance buffer hands the most recently released slot out first. With nothing selected it uploads nothing, not even its camera. With the engine in a worker the select tool still runs on the page: the model is plain data, sent with `EngineHost.setOverlay`, and `createSelectionOverlayLayer` draws it in the worker with this same class, at each frame's zoom. **Large selections**: the rectangles are rebuilt at every zoom a glide draws, and select all on 50,000 loose objects meant 200,000 outline rectangles each such frame (and a 50,000-box model posted to the worker per selection change). Above `MAX_OUTLINED_OBJECTS` (500) the model carries no outlines but the selection's count (`countBadge`), which the overlay draws as a badge below the frame round everything, the same blue (the lock colour for a locked selection) with white seven-segment digits (the overlay has no text). The badge stays 8 px inside the viewport: below the frame, else above it, else pinned to the viewport's bottom edge under the frame's centre (an unturned frame's badge also slides sideways), so after select all and a zoom in the count is still on screen; the overlay rebuilds on a pan only while a badge shows. The select tool finds the badge where the overlay draws it (`SelectController.handle` takes the camera, which the app passes) and a press on it is inert: no rotation, marquee, move or deselect (`isOnCountBadge`, press target `badge`); building the model then reads no object's box, and `overlayRectsOf` also treats a model carrying more outlines than the cap as capped, so the rebuild is O(min(selection, cap)) wherever the model came from. `selection-overlay.bench.ts` measures both at 10,000 selected and at the cap; the golden scene `selection-overlay-capped` draws it on a GPU.
- **Align, distribute, tidy up** act on the movable selection: locked objects neither move nor count towards the bounds, an object whose ancestor is selected moves with it, and connectors and comments (no box of their own) are skipped. Each object counts by the axis-aligned box round its turned corners, taken in the common parent's frame, so objects inside a turned frame line up along the frame's own axes; a selection spanning several parents is arranged on the board's axes and each object is moved in its own parent (the move carries no `parentId`, so's reparent never fires). One object aligns to the frame it is in; at the root or in a group it stays. Distribution orders by centre and keeps the outer two; tidy up reads rows top to bottom (a box lying wholly below the current row starts the next), places each box at the top-left of its cell from the bounds' corner, and spaces cells by the median of today's gaps. Whatever the operation, it is one `moveObjects` command: one transaction, one change set, one undo step; nothing is written when nothing would move.
- **Touch**. One finger drives the tool as the mouse does: a tap is a click, a drag moves, resizes, rotates or draws a marquee, after 8 px of travel (`clickThresholdPx`, the long-press slop) rather than 4. A finger's targets are larger but biased out of the frame (`hitHandle` and `hitRotationBand` take the pointer type): each handle's pickable square is 44 CSS px, reaching into the frame at most 6 px, or a sixth of the frame's on-screen side on that axis if less, and 38 px or more out of it, so at least the middle two thirds of a small selected object along each axis are the object's and a finger there moves it; the rotation band runs 24 px beyond the corner squares. Drawn handles, and the mouse's and pen's targets, are unchanged. Every input carries the DOM event's `timeStamp` (on `performance.now`'s clock), the one clock of both the double press and the long-press: the app's timer sends `hold` with the time and the finger's place, and a touch press still `pressing`, held `LONG_PRESS_MS` and not 8 px from where it landed (a press on the inert count badge can wander without dragging), emits `contextMenu` and leaves the tool at rest, so a menu entry runs even while the finger is down (the app ignores the rest of that finger). A secondary click (`contextPress`) resolves like a press and opens the menu at once; on empty board the selection is cleared first. A second finger landing is a `pointerCancel`: a drag drops its preview and writes nothing, and a press puts back the selection its first finger changed. A finger's double-tap is the same double press as a mouse's double click (`isDoublePress`, 32 px instead of 4, 400 ms), but it is decided on the second lift, not the second press: the second tap carries `activate` (or, on empty board, `doubleTap`) as its click, so a second press that drags past the 8 px slop is a drag and one held 500 ms is a long-press, and neither activates nor zooms. A mouse's and a pen's double press still activate at the second press. `activate` carries the pointer type, and `activationRoute` sends it to `enterGroup` for a group not entered, to `onActivate` for an object with a mode of its own (`canActivate`), and only otherwise to `onDoubleTap('selection')`; a finger's second tap on empty board carries `doubleTap` (fit the selection, or the board when nothing is selected), neither clearing nor leaving a group. A frame's background counts as empty board here, as it does for a click, so a double-tap there fits the board. So a double-tap never both activates and zooms. `select-tool.ts` keeps the steps of a press; its state (`select-tool-state.ts`), the click threshold (`click-threshold.ts`) and the context menu steps (`context-menu-steps.ts`) are files of their own.
- **Lines and arrows**. The line tool and endpoint editing share one rule for where an end goes (`lineEndAt`): Shift wins and turns the end to the nearest 45° about the other end; otherwise, with snapping on (the default) and Alt not held, an object anchor within 8 CSS px wins over edge-and-centre and grid snapping. Anchors are the centre and edge midpoints of each object's select box (turned with it); lines offer none, since their ends are free points and a line attached to objects is a connector, so landing on an anchor attaches nothing. The anchor query is one R-tree search of the pointer grown by 8 CSS px (an anchor lies on its object's box, so the box meets it), never a scan of the board, and measures up to `ANCHOR_CANDIDATES_CAP` (256) proposed objects exactly, the nearest anchor winning; only a larger pile is first cut to the cap by the anchors of the objects' indexed boxes, an object without an indexed box ranking last (fix round 1: at a cap of 64 ranked by indexed boxes, a turned square whose real edge midpoint was under the pointer could rank behind 64 large frames and be dropped). `line-anchors.bench.ts`: a few µs beside an object on a 50k board, about 0.20 ms mean over 500 piled objects with 256 measured. A drawn line sits at the root with its origin on its first end; a drag of a selected line's end previews the line and writes one `setLinePoints` of that end, in board coordinates, on release, so a line in a rotated group follows the pointer exactly. The drag keeps the line's id, not the line: every move reads the line again, so a collaborator's move of the line or drag of its other end shows in the preview, and the release writes only the dragged end (each end is its own key in the document), leaving the other end where the collaborator put it; a line deleted meanwhile ends the drag. The lone selected line shows its two end handles instead of the frame and its box handles (the overlay's `frame` is replaced); a locked line shows its locked frame and no handles. A select tool made with `hasLineEndHandles` (the app's) gives such a line no frame controls at all (`SelectToolContext.hasFrameControls`, cached in `SelectionFrameCache`): no resize handles and no rotation bands, since its frame is the axis-aligned box of its ends, whose two other corners are empty board, where a press draws a marquee (fix round 1: the hidden bands turned the line). A line turns by dragging an end. On a line shorter on screen than two handle squares (a finger's are 44 px), the squares shrink so each covers at most a third of the line: the middle third stays body, which a press drags whole. A line shorter on screen than half a square, down to one whose ends coincide after a merge, keeps whole squares (the end handle on top), so an end can be pulled out again. A click, a cancel (a second finger, a lost pointer) and Escape write nothing; the arrow sub-tool is the line tool with an `arrow` end cap in its style (the app's toolbar shows the sub-tool).
- **Tool modes and activation**. Every tool other than select is a `ToolMode` (`src/modes/tool-mode.ts`): its pure core lives in its own area here, DOM-free, and the app's `ToolRouter` gives it the primary button and the canvas keys while it is active, the select tool taking them otherwise and whatever a mode declines or passes on when it finishes. A mode shows transient geometry (a caret, a text selection, snap guides, pen handles) by filling `SelectionOverlayModel.toolRects`, flat oriented rectangles in world units drawn after everything else, so it reaches the engine worker with the rest of the model. The select tool emits `activate` for a double press on an object (`isDoublePress`), decided on the second release for every pointer: released without passing the dead zone it activates, while a second press that drags moves the object, so clicking a shape and at once dragging it moves it rather than opening its label's editor; and for Enter with one object selected at rest (at the frame's centre, marked `source: 'enter'`); a group not yet entered is entered, and any other object goes through `SelectControllerOptions.onActivate` to the router, which starts the mode registered for the object's type.
- **Pen tool and vector editing**. Handles belong to segment ends; a vertex's `mirror` couples its two handles only while exactly two segment ends meet there. The pen's chain, its outgoing handle, a press under way and the chain's vertex while no segment joins it are mode state (`pen-held-vertex.ts`): the document hides a vertex no segment joins once the path has a segment, and a path of lone vertices is invisible, so the pen creates a path with its first segment, Escape after one click writes nothing (nothing to undo), and a Backspace that takes a path's last segment deletes the path and holds its first point in a draft in the same frame; the network after a press is previewed (`setPreview`) and written on release as one command, so a drag writes once, a second finger (`pointerCancel`) writes nothing, and each press is one undo step. The dead zone is measured from where the press landed (3 CSS px), so a press a few px off a vertex with a pixel of jitter is still a click, and nothing a pick-up seeded (smooth continuation) is overwritten inside it. Member ids come from the command environment's `createId`, so two clients adding vertices at once never share an id; the document keys members by id and merges edits of different members, and a read of the path leaves out what a merge leaves malformed, keeping it stored (the document README, "Vector networks"). **Alt:** `ToolPointerInput` carries Alt as `isSnapSuspended` (the snap modifier). The pen reads it as "break the mirror" while it places a point or drags a handle, vector editing as "break the mirror" on a handle drag and "toggle corner and smooth" on a vertex click; neither suspends snapping, which only the pen's new points use (`createPenSnapper`, to objects on screen and the grid; Shift's 45° steps win over snapping). The two modes are sub-tools of one session: `ToolMode.onToolSwitch` lets V and P hand the path over without ending it, and vector editing reports the select tool's id, so the toolbar follows the sub-tool. Both modes' overlays draw every segment as a 1 px line (`networkOverlayRects`), so a path with no stroke and no fill shows while it is edited; vector editing draws the segment under the pointer 2 px wide (not while the pointer is on a vertex) and asks for the prototype's cursors: `pointer` over a vertex or handle, `move` over a segment and for the whole of a drag (`ToolCursor` gains both). **Hover cost** (fix round 2): a hover hit test measures only the curves whose control-point box is near the pointer (`segment-bounds.ts`, the boxes kept per network object); vector editing signals a change only when the hovered segment, the cursor, the zoom or the culled region changed, and returns its pointer steps as shown (`isShown`), so the router does not rebuild the overlay after each move; its skeleton is kept (`network-skeleton.ts`: the polylines per network, frame and half-octave zoom bucket, the rectangles per exact zoom and region) and culled with the vertices to the viewport grown by half its size each side, the viewport coming from pointer inputs and `refreshCursor`, which also re-hovers under a still pointer after a camera move (the whole skeleton while no camera has been seen; during a fit's glide the region is the destination's). The engine still re-meshes the whole previewed path on each drag move (a follow-up). **Stacking with notes:** a path reports its box (its control points' hull, `worldBoundsOf`) to the engine's z-order segment planner through the culler's index, as a line does, so a stroke over a sticky note draws over it and a note over a stroke hides it (golden scene `pen-and-notes`); known limit: within one segment the text pass draws after the path pass, so a stroke over a note falls under the note's text. The golden scene `pen-paths` covers the lines, the hovered one and an unstroked path. What is left out: snapping of vertex and handle drags.
- **Rich text editing** (ported from the prototype's `src/text/runs.ts` and `src/text/editor.ts`, design `webgl-whiteboard/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-19-rich-text-runs-design.md`). `src/text/runs/` holds the pure run operations over the schema's `StyleRun`, every result meeting `findRunIssues` (run boundaries between code points included). The editor (`editor-state.ts`, `editor.ts`, `editor-navigation.ts`) keeps text, runs, caret, anchor and a pending style; `replaceRange` is its single mutation; the insert style is the first selected character's, or the character before the caret's except at a line start with a character after it, then the pending style (set by a style change at a collapsed caret, kept through typing, cleared by any caret move or delete); `beginComposition` deletes a selection but pins its style so a composition over it keeps it. `TextMode` is the text tool's `ToolMode`: its `TextEditingSession` writes every edit at once as one `setTextRuns` with the box's measured `size` (laid out on the page with the engine's faces through `TextLayoutService`), so collaborators see typing live; typing joins one undo step per word through `TypingCapture` (a space or newline typed, or a caret jump, ends it), other edits are a step each; the document's changes to the text (a collaborator, an undo) move the caret with the text around it (`followDocument`); an input method's composition is shown as a preview object (never written) and committed as one edit. The session edits text objects and sticky notes alike (below, "Sticky notes"). A box the text tool makes is a draft (`TextSessionObject`: its id chosen at once, its caret and frame shown from local state) until its first committed character, which is one `createObject` carrying the text, runs and measured size, in the first word's undo step; so a click ended with Escape writes nothing and leaves no undo step. Nothing is written with a size measured without fonts: while `TextLayoutService` answers nothing, a draft's content is held (`TextSessionObject.isHeld`) and created, measured, by `TextEditingSession.writeHeld` once `whenReady` settles (or with its placeholder size when the session ends first), and a text written without a layout keeps its size; both are reported through `TextModeContext.onWrittenUnmeasured` for the app to measure later. Text the session created and emptied again is deleted within its typing step; other text committed empty is deleted as a step of its own. A collaborator deleting the text ends the session with nothing written; a collaborator locking it ends the session with a notice (`onNotice`, `LOCKED_WHILE_EDITING_NOTICE`), what was typed already written, and `setTextRuns` refuses the locked object (`onCommandRefused`). The caret, selection highlight, composition underline and the box outline are `toolRects`, turned with the object, and the selection frame hides while editing.

- **Sticky notes** (ported from the prototype's `src/board/note.ts` and `src/app/controller.ts`, design `webgl-whiteboard/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-21-sticky-notes-design.md`). A note is a text box: the editor (`TextEditingSession`, `TextMode`) edits any `TextBoxObject`, and everything that maps between the world and the laid-out text goes through where the engine's text pass places it (`textOriginIn`, over the engine's `textPlacementOf` and `textOriginOf`), so the caret, the highlights and a press's caret position sit on the glyphs the engine draws: a note's text wraps inside its 16-unit padding and short text is centred vertically as well as horizontally (the note's `align` and `verticalAlign`, which the tool sets to `center` and `middle`). Each edit writes the note's measured size through `setTextRuns`' `size`: its width kept, its height at least square and taller once the text needs it (`measuredSize` over the engine's `noteHeightFor`), so a note grows downwards as it is typed into. A note only grows: typing, a restyle or a remote change never makes it shorter than it is, so a note the person made taller than its text (a resize) keeps that height while typed into, and never goes below square. The editor outlines and hit-tests the box the engine draws (`editedBoxSize`: the stored height, or the text with its padding once taller) and places the caret by the stored height as the engine does (`textOriginIn`), so on a 200 × 400 note with short text the caret sits on the glyphs centred at y = 200 and a press on the lower half moves the caret rather than ending the edit. An empty note is kept when editing ends (the paper is the object), where empty text is deleted; a note is created at once by the tool, rather than lazily with its first character as text is, because an empty note is a thing on the board. `StickyMode` is the tool: until a note is made a click (released anywhere, created at the press) makes one centred on it, then every input goes to a `TextMode` editing it, so a press outside commits and passes the press on (the router then makes select active, as after the text tool) and Escape selects the note. Selecting does not raise, so "raise carries labels" is `carriedLabelsOf`, applied by the app to bring to front and bring forward only: text below a note is hidden by it already, and after a send backward the text on a note stays above it by itself. To the front it carries every text above a selected note and on it; forward it carries only the text the note would otherwise fall below (a run of selected siblings hops over the sibling directly above it, so that sibling is carried when it is text on a note of the run, and the grown run goes on to the next), so with the stack note, other, label, other the label stays where it is. It walks each selected note's parent's children once: to the front, text below the lowest selected note is passed over with one comparison and the rest is tested only against the notes its box overlaps (`NoteBoxGrid`, a uniform grid of the notes' boxes, about √S cells a side), about O((T + S) log S + matches) with T texts and S selected notes; forward sorts the children once. `carried-labels.bench.ts` (50,000 siblings, 13,500 notes and 5,000 texts; Apple M1 Max, machine shared, so rough): to the front with 1,000 notes selected 3.8 ms (was 173 ms), with all 13,500 10.1 ms (was 2.8 s); forward with all 13,500 18.4 ms.
- **Shapes**. `ShapeMode` is the shape tool. A drag previews the shape it will create as the object itself (`setPreview`, `DRAFT_PREVIEW_Z`, under the id `createObject` then keeps), so the preview is the SDF shape the engine draws; the fixed corner is the press (snapped), the other the pointer; Shift squares the box toward the pointer and Alt, the key that also suspends snapping, draws it about the press, and neither snaps (a snapped corner would be moved again by the constraint). A press released within the click threshold makes a 100-unit shape centred on it. Creation is one `createObject`, so one undo step; the shape is selected and the mode reports it is done, so the select tool takes over, as the text and sticky tools hand back. The kind, fill, outline, corner radius and sides come from the tool's style (`newShapeStyle`), so the last kind picked in the panel is the next shape's. A label is edited by the same text editor as a text box: the editor takes an `EditableTextObject`, and a shape's label is laid out, placed, outlined and hit-tested by the engine's own placement (`shapeLabelPlacement`) from the shape's stored size, the whole shape being the editor's box, and a press on label lines that overflow it above or below (a label taller than its box stays centred) placing the caret too (`editedHitRect`); a label has no size of its own, so `setTextRuns` writes it without one and it is never reported unmeasured, and an emptied label leaves the shape as it was. A shape without a label starts with empty text whose one run is the typing style (`newTextStyle`, the shape tool's label style), and the first character creates the label.

Runs in the browser (DOM lib), though nothing in `src/camera/`, `src/select/`, `src/arrange/` or `src/sticky/` uses the DOM.

### Related selection changes

`SelectionFrameCache` retains each selected object's bound verdict, oriented box and world bounds in `SelectionFarCache`. A change invalidates the verdicts that depend on the changed object, an ancestor, or a selected container's descendants (including new arrivals and reparenting). The aggregate frame scans cached bounds and recomputes geometry only for invalidated objects; flags are recomputed, and resize handles reuse the scope's near ids. Selecting again or ending a gesture clears the bound cache. Its lifetime and entries are bounded by the current selection. `selection-frame-cache.test.ts` compares cached geometry with fresh calculations through moves, reparenting, arrivals, deletion, far transitions and drawable-gate changes. `selection-change.bench.ts` measures a real store change and overlay update with 10,000 selected rectangles.
